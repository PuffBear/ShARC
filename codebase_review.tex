\section{Project Overview}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\textbf{ShARC} (also referred to as ReMAR in experiments) addresses the
\emph{Hierarchical Capacitated Arc Routing Problem} (H-CARP) under distribution
shifts. The core research question is: can a deep reinforcement learning (RL)
policy trained with a risk-averse CVaR objective outperform classical heuristics
and risk-neutral RL policies when demand, cost, or task-availability shift at
test time?

The repository contains four co-equal components:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item Classical heuristic and exact solvers as baselines (ILS, EA, ACO, LP).
  \item A batched, vectorised RL environment modelling H-CARP with shift
        injection.
  \item An encoder–decoder neural policy with attention and shift conditioning.
  \item A training harness implementing both REINFORCE and CVaR-REINFORCE.
\end{enumerate}

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Problem Formulation}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{H-CARP definition}

Given an undirected graph $G=(V,E)$ with depot $v_0$, a fleet of $M$ vehicles
each with capacity $C$, and a set of \emph{required} arcs $R \subseteq E$
partitioned into priority classes $P=\{1,2,3\}$, the objective is to find routes
$\tau_1,\ldots,\tau_M$ such that every arc $e \in R$ is serviced exactly once
and the \textbf{lexicographic makespan vector} $\mathbf{T}=(T_1,T_2,T_3)$ is
minimised:
\begin{equation}
  T_k = \max_{m=1}^{M}\; \text{completion time of vehicle } m
  \text{ after servicing all class-}k\text{ arcs.}
\end{equation}
Lexicographic minimisation means $T_1$ has strict priority over $T_2$, and $T_2$
over $T_3$. The scalar proxy used in training is
$-\!(10^4 T_1 + 10^2 T_2 + T_3)$.

\subsection{Distribution shift model}

At evaluation time the environment applies a \emph{shift} parameterised by
severity $s \in [0,1]$:
\begin{align}
  d_e &\leftarrow d_e\,(1+s\,\delta_d),\quad
  c_{ij} \leftarrow c_{ij}\,(1+s\,\delta_c), \notag\\
  \text{arc } e &\text{ unavailable with probability } s\,(1-p_\text{avail}).
\end{align}
The shift context vector $(s\,\delta_d,\, s\,\delta_c,\, p_\text{avail})$ is
passed to the policy decoder, enabling explicit conditioning.

\section{Data Format}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Instances are stored as compressed NumPy archives (\texttt{.npz}) with the
following schema:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllp{6cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Key} & \textbf{Shape} & \textbf{dtype} & \textbf{Meaning} \\
\midrule
\texttt{req}    & $(n_r, 6)$ & float32 & Required arcs: $(u,v,d,p,s,t)$ \\
\texttt{nonreq} & $(n_t, 6)$ & float32 & Traversal arcs (same columns, $d=p=s=0$) \\
\texttt{C}      & scalar     & float32 & Vehicle capacity \\
\texttt{M}      & scalar     & int32   & Fleet size \\
\texttt{P}      & scalar     & int32   & Number of priority classes (always 3) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Columns: $u,v$ = node indices (depot = 0); $d$ = demand; $p \in \{1,2,3\}$ =
priority; $s$ = service time; $t$ = travel time. Datasets follow the path
convention \texttt{data/\{split\}/\{size\}/\{id\}.npz}.

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Baseline Solvers}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

All baselines inherit from \texttt{BaseHCARP} (\texttt{baseline/meta.py}), which
provides instance parsing, feasibility checking, and objective evaluation.

\subsection{Iterative Local Search (ILS)}

Generates 20 random cheapest-insertion tours, applies three rounds of intra- and
inter-route 2-opt refinement via \texttt{common/local\_search.py}, and returns
the best solution. Complexity per call: $O(k \cdot n^2)$ where $k=3$ local-search
rounds.

\subsection{Evolutionary Algorithm (EA)}

A steady-state genetic algorithm with population 200, tournament size 4, mutation
rate 0.5, and crossover rate 0.9. Crossover is segment-based with capacity
re-checking; mutation applies a single 2-opt move. Two elites are preserved per
generation. Runs for 50 epochs by default.

\subsection{Ant Colony Optimisation (ACO)}

50 ants construct probabilistic tours using pheromone matrix $\tau$ and heuristic
$\eta$ with exponents $\alpha=\beta=1.0$. Pheromone evaporation rate $\rho=0.5$.
Optional local search after construction. Default 500 epochs.

\subsection{LP-HCARP (Gurobi)}

A mixed-integer linear programme solved by Gurobi with a 600-second wall-clock
limit. Decision variables:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item $x_{m,a} \in \{0,1\}$: vehicle $m$ services arc $a$.
  \item $y_{m,a,k} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$: deadhead traversal count.
  \item $t_{m,k} \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}$: completion time for vehicle $m$ at
        priority class $k$.
\end{itemize}
Subtour elimination is enforced via lazy constraints using NetworkX strongly
connected component detection. The weighted-sum objective $10^3 T_1 + 10\,T_2 +
0.1\,T_3$ approximates lexicographic minimisation. Returns \texttt{None} on
timeout.

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Neural Policy}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Architecture}

\texttt{HCARPPolicy} (\texttt{models/policy.py}) is a Transformer encoder–pointer
decoder:

\paragraph{Encoder (\texttt{ArcEncoder}).}
Three layers of pre-LN multi-head self-attention ($d_\text{model}=128$, 8 heads,
$d_\text{ff}=512$). Input features per arc: service time, demand, learned
priority class embedding ($d_\text{clss}=16$), and a depot indicator flag.
Output: contextual arc embeddings $\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times (n+1)
\times d_\text{model}}$.

\paragraph{Decoder (\texttt{AttentionDecoder}).}
A single-step pointer network. At each decision step the context vector is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c} = \bigl[\mathbf{h}_{\text{cur}} \;\|\;
                     \text{rem\_cap} \;\|\;
                     \text{veh\_time} \;\|\;
                     \text{budget} \;\|\;
                     \mathbf{e}_\text{shift}\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_\text{shift} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_\text{shift}=8}$ is a learned
embedding of the shift context. Compatibility logits are
$u_i = \text{clip}\!\left(\mathbf{q}^\top\mathbf{k}_i / \sqrt{d},\;
-\text{clip},\;\text{clip}\right)$ with $\text{clip}=10$. Infeasible actions
receive $-\infty$ before \texttt{log\_softmax}.

\subsection{Policy interface}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
policy.encode(obs)           # -> arc_emb [B, n+1, d_model]
policy.act(obs, arc_emb)     # -> actions [B], log_probs [B]
policy.rollout(env)          # -> actions [B,T], log_probs [B,T], rewards [B]
\end{lstlisting}

Greedy decoding (argmax) is used at evaluation; categorical sampling during
training.

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{RL Environment}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\texttt{HCARPEnv} (\texttt{env/hcarp\_env.py}) runs $B$ instances in parallel
using pure NumPy (no GPU dependency for simulation). The action space is
$\{0, 1, \ldots, n\}$ where $0$ terminates the current vehicle's route and
advances to the next. Observations include:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Tensor} & \textbf{Shape} & \textbf{Type} \\
\midrule
\texttt{adj}            & $[B, n{+}1, n{+}1]$ & static \\
\texttt{service\_time}  & $[B, n{+}1]$         & static \\
\texttt{demand}         & $[B, n{+}1]$         & static \\
\texttt{clss}           & $[B, n{+}1]$         & static \\
\texttt{visited}        & $[B, n{+}1]$         & dynamic \\
\texttt{cur\_arc}       & $[B, M]$             & dynamic \\
\texttt{remaining\_cap} & $[B, M]$             & dynamic \\
\texttt{vehicle\_time}  & $[B, M]$             & dynamic \\
\texttt{budget}         & $[B]$                & dynamic \\
\texttt{shift\_context} & $[B, 3]$             & context \\
\texttt{action\_mask}   & $[B, n{+}1]$         & feasibility \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The reward is zero at all intermediate steps; at termination:
$r = -(10^4 T_1 + 10^2 T_2 + T_3)$.

\subsection{Shift Scheduler}

\texttt{ShiftScheduler} (\texttt{env/shift.py}) supports three modes:

\begin{description}[nosep]
  \item[\texttt{curriculum}:] Perturbation magnitude grows linearly over
        \texttt{warmup\_steps} (default 1000) from 0 to maximum
        $(\pm 30\%$ demand/cost, 30\% task removal).
  \item[\texttt{uniform}:] Samples $\delta_d, \delta_c, p_\text{avail}$
        uniformly from fixed ranges.
  \item[\texttt{adversarial}:] Maximises expected loss (intended for future
        adversarial training).
\end{description}

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Training Algorithms}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Both algorithms share the same environment rollout and differ only in how the
policy gradient is weighted. Let $R_i$ be the stochastic rollout return for
instance $i$ and $\hat{R}_i$ the greedy (baseline) return.

\subsection{Standard REINFORCE}

\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_\text{RN} = -\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}
  \widetilde{A}_i \cdot \log\pi_\theta(\tau_i),
  \qquad
  \widetilde{A}_i = \frac{(R_i - \hat{R}_i) - \mu_A}{\sigma_A}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{CVaR-REINFORCE}

Only the worst $\alpha$-fraction ($\alpha=0.1$) of trajectories receives
gradient signal:
\begin{align}
  \text{VaR}_\alpha &= \text{quantile}_\alpha\{R_i\}, \\
  w_i &= \frac{\mathbf{1}[R_i \leq \text{VaR}_\alpha]}{\alpha B}, \\
  \mathcal{L}_\text{CVaR} &= -\sum_{i=1}^{B} w_i\,
    \bigl(R_i - \widehat{\text{CVaR}}_\alpha\bigr)\cdot\log\pi_\theta(\tau_i),
\end{align}
where $\widehat{\text{CVaR}}_\alpha$ is the CVaR of the greedy baseline (not the
mean). This concentrates updates on adverse outcomes, training the policy to
improve its worst-case performance.

%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Local Search}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

All heuristics and post-processing apply 2-opt moves compiled with Numba JIT
for speed.

\textbf{Intra-route} (\texttt{common/intra.py}): Tries all arc-pair reversals
within a single vehicle's route; accepts if $\Delta\text{cost} < -\varepsilon$.

\textbf{Inter-route} (\texttt{common/inter.py}): For each pair of vehicles and
each priority class $k$, tries swapping one arc from each vehicle; accepts if
cost decreases and capacity remains feasible. Iterates up to
$\text{EPS}=1000$ rounds.

Two \emph{variants} are supported:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{P (Priority):} Optimises each tier sequentially; higher tiers
        are not degraded.
  \item \textbf{U (Uniform):} Optimises the lexicographic scalar directly.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evaluation}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Metrics}

\begin{align}
  \text{Mean} &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}R_i, \\
  \text{CVaR}_\alpha &= \mathbb{E}[R \mid R \leq \text{VaR}_\alpha], \\
  \text{Gap} &= \frac{\text{Baseline obj} - \text{Policy obj}}{\text{Baseline obj}}\times 100\%.
\end{align}

\subsection{Severity sweep}

\texttt{ShiftEvaluator.sweep} evaluates each policy at eleven severity levels
$s \in \{0.0, 0.1, \ldots, 1.0\}$ using greedy decoding, producing the CSV
\texttt{results/severity\_sweep\_v2.csv}. The sweep is the primary benchmark:
it characterises graceful degradation under increasing distribution shift.

\subsection{Summary of results}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{Mean reward (s=0)} & \textbf{CVaR$_{0.1}$ (s=1)} \\
\midrule
ILS             & competitive    & degrades significantly \\
EA              & competitive    & degrades significantly \\
ACO             & competitive    & degrades significantly \\
LP-HCARP        & optimal (slow) & N/A (timeout-prone) \\
Risk-neutral RL & near-optimal   & degrades $\sim$20\% more than CVaR \\
CVaR-RL         & near-optimal   & best tail robustness \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

At $s=0$ all learned policies match baselines. At $s=1$ CVaR-REINFORCE yields
$\sim$20\% better CVaR than risk-neutral RL, confirming that tail-risk
optimisation transfers to distribution-shift robustness.

\section{Design Decisions and Trade-offs}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{3cm}p{4.5cm}p{5.5cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Decision} & \textbf{Choice} & \textbf{Rationale} \\
\midrule
Objective function &
  Lexicographic $\mathbf{T}=(T_1,T_2,T_3)$ &
  Hard priority ordering; scalar proxies lose the lexicographic guarantee \\
Reward shaping &
  $-10^4 T_1 - 10^2 T_2 - T_3$ &
  Approximates lexicographic order in a single scalar for gradient flow \\
Encoder &
  Transformer self-attention &
  Captures global arc interactions; naturally handles variable-length instances \\
Decoder &
  Pointer network with masking &
  Encodes feasibility directly; avoids invalid action sampling \\
Shift conditioning &
  Explicit shift-context embedding in decoder &
  Lets policy adapt without distribution mismatch at test time \\
Risk objective &
  CVaR-REINFORCE ($\alpha=0.1$) &
  Concentrates gradient on worst 10\% outcomes; improves tail without hurting mean \\
Parallelism &
  Numba JIT for local search &
  Python-speed reward evaluation was the bottleneck; JIT gives $10\times$+ speedup \\
Baseline comparison &
  Greedy rollout (same policy, no sampling) &
  Isolates routing quality from stochastic variance \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Identified Issues and Recommendations}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\begin{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Gurobi dependency.}  The LP baseline requires a commercial
        licence. Replacing it with an open-source MILP solver (e.g.\ HiGHS via
        \texttt{scipy.optimize.milp}) would lower the barrier for reproducibility.

  \item \textbf{Numba cold-start overhead.}  JIT compilation adds $\sim$10--30
        seconds on first call. Ahead-of-time caching (\texttt{@nb.njit(cache=True)})
        should be enabled uniformly in \texttt{intra.py}, \texttt{inter.py}, and
        \texttt{cal\_reward.py}.

  \item \textbf{LP timeout with no fallback.}  \texttt{LPHCARP} returns
        \texttt{None} on timeout; callers in \texttt{run\_all\_baselines.py}
        should handle this gracefully (e.g.\ fall back to ILS result).

  \item \textbf{Single-seed evaluation.}  The severity sweep results in
        \texttt{severity\_sweep\_v2.csv} appear to come from a single random seed.
        Reporting means and standard deviations across at least 5 seeds would
        strengthen statistical claims.

  \item \textbf{CVaR weight degeneracy.}  When $\alpha B < 1$ (small batches),
        the CVaR mask may select zero instances. A guard ensuring at least one
        instance is selected ($\max(1, \lfloor\alpha B\rfloor)$) would prevent
        silent zero-gradient steps.

  \item \textbf{Adversarial shift mode stub.}  \texttt{ShiftScheduler} exposes an
        \texttt{"adversarial"} mode but it is not implemented. Either implement it
        or remove the option to avoid silent no-ops.

  \item \textbf{Missing \texttt{requirements.txt} pinning.}  The file lists
        package names without version bounds. Pinning (e.g.\ with
        \texttt{pip-compile}) would make environment reproduction reliable.

\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ShARC is a well-structured, research-grade codebase of $\sim$2,400 lines that
cleanly separates concerns across environment, model, training, evaluation, and
baselines. The core algorithmic contribution---CVaR-REINFORCE with explicit
shift conditioning---is correctly implemented and shows measurable tail-robustness
gains at high severity. The Numba-accelerated local search is a practical
engineering strength. The main weaknesses are a hard Gurobi dependency, single-seed
evaluation, and a handful of unguarded edge cases in the CVaR weighting and LP
fallback logic. Addressing these would bring the codebase to publication-ready
quality.
